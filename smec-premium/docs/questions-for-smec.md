# Questions for SMEC

Everything on this list blocks something specific. Each one says what is
waiting on it, so it can be answered out of order — and several can be
answered "no" or "drop it" just as usefully as "yes".

Grouped by who has to answer, because that is usually what decides how long
it takes.

---

## 1. Management — decisions, not documents

**1.1 Production domain — answered: `smecoilandgas.com`.** ✓

Because it is the domain the current site already uses, the switch is a
replacement rather than a move. Three things still need a yes, but they are
cutover decisions rather than questions about the domain:

- Seven WordPress and WooCommerce leftovers (`/cart`, `/checkout`,
  `/my-account`, `/thank-you`, `/sample-page`, `/hi`, `/test`) plus three
  Elementor template fragments start returning 404 where they return 200
  today. That is deliberate, but it happens on a live domain.
- 263 legacy image URLs break unless the `/wp-content/uploads/` folder is
  copied across first — see 4.4.
- There is no period where both sites are reachable, so we should agree a
  rollback plan before the switch. Staging will need its own subdomain.

**1.2 Where should an RFQ land — a mailbox, or a CRM?**
The form is built and tested; it needs a destination. Also confirm the public
phone number and whether there is a WhatsApp business number to publish.

This may be closer to answered than it looks. All four `smec.in` landing
pages (5.4) already post to `https://api.smec.in/api/leads/submit/` — the
same endpoint this build targets by default. So the lead backend is live and
taking enquiries in production today; what we need is the campaign key and
confirmation of who receives them.

Worth asking in the same breath whether that endpoint returns an enquiry
reference. The 2026 homepage document requires a `SMEC-RFQ-XXXX` reference
"generated by the live system", which a front end cannot invent safely. If
the API already issues one, that requirement is solved rather than built.
*Blocks: the enquiry form going live.*

**1.3 Who hosts it, and who owns the repository?**
This decides two things in the build: whether the RFQ form can keep its
server-side validation and rate limiting (it needs a Node runtime), and how
staging is password-protected. Staging must not be publicly crawlable.
*Blocks: launch, staging protection, the RFQ endpoint's final shape.*

**1.4 Who writes the content after launch, and in what?**
The handover requires that non-technical staff can edit titles, meta
descriptions, H1s, answer blocks, FAQs and CTAs. Today content is a generated
file and every edit needs a developer. A CMS is a platform decision before it
is a build task — but the decision has to be made.
*Blocks: the "content can be edited" acceptance criterion.*

**1.5 Are we launching English-only?**
Assumed yes. Confirming it means no hreflang, no Arabic variant, and no
country pages written only to target a country.
*Blocks: nothing today; closes an item.*

**1.6 The three pages with no home in the new architecture —
`/sustainability/`, `/research-and-developement/` (ADAM-Edge) and the ADIPEC
event pages. Keep them, or retire them?**
They are live on the current site and have no place in the new structure.
Right now they are reachable but excluded from search. Keeping them means
writing them into the architecture; retiring them means a 410 and a note in
the redirect map. Either is fine — leaving them undecided is not.
*Blocks: the redirect map being final.*

## 4. IT / analytics

**4.1 Who owns GA4, Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and what
are the IDs?**
All eleven conversion events are built and firing into the data layer, behind
a consent gate. They go nowhere until a container ID exists.

Two things about *owns*, because they are hard to undo later. The properties
should be created inside SMEC's own Google account, with the developer and
any agency added as users — not the other way round. A property created in
someone else's account takes its history with it when that relationship ends.
And if an agency is running search (5.6), they need access to the same
property rather than a second one; two properties measuring one site produce
two different numbers and no way to tell which is right.

The event names are also still open. The build uses the eleven names the
Technical SEO Master fixed in §19 — `rfq_submit`, `click_whatsapp`,
`click_email`, `rfq_file_upload` and the rest. The 2026 homepage document
proposes different names for the same actions (`generate_lead`,
`whatsapp_click`, `contact_email_click`, `file_upload_success`) plus four new
ones. Nothing is live yet, so this costs nothing to settle now and gets
expensive once there is history behind a name.
*Blocks: measurement from day one, and a UAT sign-off row.*

**4.2 Privacy notice, cookie wording, form consent text and a retention
policy for uploaded RFQ files.**
The consent banner and the form's consent checkbox both carry plain-English
stand-in wording written to be replaced. Uploaded drawings and specifications
are the sensitive part: how long are they kept, who can see them, and how are
they deleted?
*Blocks: launch, legally rather than technically.*

**4.3 Is there an IndexNow key, and can the host rewrite `/<key>.txt`?**
Submission is built and dormant. Optional, but cheap.
*Blocks: nothing.*

**4.4 What should happen to 263 legacy image URLs, and should the 15 product
datasheets be republished?**
The live site has been crawled and the inventory is in `legacy-inventory.md`.
The 15 datasheets and 25 images now redirect to their new homes. The
remaining 263 image URLs will 404 after cutover — copying the
`/wp-content/uploads/` folder across before launch keeps every one of them
working and is the only option that loses nothing.

On the datasheets: they are published product PDFs, and they are very likely
the approved numerical source the product pages are waiting on in 3.1. If
they are current, hosting them again is better than redirecting them.

A note on timing: copying the folder is only possible while the current site
is still serving. After the origin switches, those files are gone unless
someone took them first. This is one of the few items on the list with a
window rather than a deadline.

It is also worth separating this from the photography SMEC is sending for the
redesign. Those are new assets for new pages; the 263 are old URLs that break
regardless of how good the new photographs are. They do overlap, though — 186
of the 288 were uploaded in 2025 and 2026, on pages like
`/industrial-control-panel` and `/e-house-solutions-india`, so a good share of
them are already the real project photography rather than stock. Where SMEC
holds the originals, full-resolution copies are better than the
WordPress-compressed versions on the live site.

**Also needed: Search Console access** — see 5.2, which puts a date on it.
It is the only way to see which old URLs and images actually have traffic and
inbound links, rather than guessing from a crawl.
*Blocks: an item on the migration deliverables list, and possibly 3.1.*

## 5. Search — now that SEO is being rebuilt

SMEC has said search is being approached from scratch. Worth saying once, at
the top of this section: that is available for the *strategy*, but not for
the *domain*. `smecoilandgas.com` already carries 95 crawlable pages, 18
published articles and 288 images. Whatever launches inherits that history,
earned and unearned. These questions are mostly about making that explicit
rather than discovering it in January.

**5.1 Is keyword validation happening before routes freeze, or are the
proposed slugs accepted as-is?**
The Six-Tab Architecture document is candid that its search phrases are
"recommendations, not verified search-volume data", and asks for validation
in UAE/GCC and India using Keyword Planner, Search Console and competitor
SERP analysis. The same document's implementation order then opens with
"freeze canonical routes". Both cannot happen. If validation lands after the
freeze, the URLs move a second time, and on a same-domain replacement each
move costs another redirect hop.

This is the question most likely to cause rework, which is why it is first.
*Blocks: the URL map, and therefore the redirect map, the sitemap and every
internal link.*

**5.2 Search Console access now, and a performance export before cutover.**
Search Console keeps sixteen months. The comparison anyone will want in
January is against October, and October only exists if someone exports it
before the switch. Needed: which queries and pages bring traffic today,
which countries they come from, and which of the 263 images (4.4) actually
have inbound links rather than merely existing.

Without this there is no baseline, so "better" and "worse" are both
unprovable after launch.
*Blocks: nothing technically. Expires, which is worse than blocking.*

**5.3 Does "from scratch" include dropping the redirect map?**
Asking explicitly because it is easy to assume either way. The map is built
and verified against a crawl of the live site: 79 of 95 pages and 15 of 15
datasheets resolve in one hop. Keeping it costs nothing now. Dropping it is
not a neutral reset — it discards the inbound links the domain has
accumulated, and those are the one thing a genuinely new site cannot
manufacture.
*Blocks: the redirect map being final — the same decision as 1.6.*

**5.4 Which domain is meant to own which search terms?**
Six properties are in play, and most of them target overlapping ground. The
four `smec.in` landing pages are built in the `smec-automation-ads`
repository and all four return 200 today:

| Property | What it targets today |
| --- | --- |
| `smecoilandgas.com` | the new site |
| `asset-digital-intelligence.smec.in` | umbrella — "Oil & Gas, Marine & Industrial Plants \| SMEC India & **Middle East**". Covers all four platforms |
| `ai-video-surveillance.smec.in` | **NexView specifically** — "AI CCTV & Video Surveillance for Oil, Gas & Marine Sites". Own canonical, sitemap, schema and `llms.txt` |
| `industrial-control-panels.smec.in` | "Industrial Control Systems & Automation" |
| `marine-electrical-solutions.smec.in` | "Marine Electrical Solutions… **India · UAE · Middle East**" |
| `smec.in` | the group site |

Three things follow, and none of them are in the architecture document.

**NexView exists on two pages, deliberately.** The architecture routes it to
`asset-digital-intelligence.smec.in`, which is the right call — that page is
built as a four-platform umbrella ("Not four disconnected products"), and it
gives NexWave, NexView, ProSet360 and NexVerse an H3 section each with their
own proof points. It is not a page that merely mentions them.

`ai-video-surveillance.smec.in` is a different kind of page: a focused NexView
conversion page — "CCTV records. It doesn't watch", PPE and intrusion
detection, book a site assessment. Both are legitimate and serve different
intent.

The only open point is that the two never reference each other, and the
architecture table routes all four platform rows to the same umbrella URL. If
the main site ever wants to send AI-video intent somewhere specific, the
dedicated page is the better target. A refinement, not a correction.

**Fuel Monitoring exists nowhere in the new plan.** It is absent from the
architecture document, and the umbrella page does not mention it once — the
other four platforms appear 20–23 times each. Deleting
`/digital/fuel-monitoring/` removes it from SMEC's web presence entirely.

**None of the four landing pages link to each other, or to
`smecoilandgas.com`.** The architecture document's rule — "external platform →
back-links to relevant SMEC industry and enquiry pages where appropriate" —
describes something that does not happen today in either direction. They are
four isolated islands.

The headline consequence stays the same: a search rebuild for
`smecoilandgas.com` would begin by handing its own Middle East digitalisation
terms to other domains, and the seven `/digital/` pages already built would be
deleted rather than launched. Marine is the same question a second time, since
the new architecture adds `/industries/marine-offshore-vessels` while
`marine-electrical-solutions.smec.in` already targets that market.

That may well be the right call commercially. It should be a decision rather
than a side effect.
*Blocks: whether the Digitalisation cluster is built or removed, and the
marine industry page.*

**5.5 The 58 LinkedIn articles — can they be republished here?**
Projects & Resources currently resolves to 58 links, every one pointing at
LinkedIn: 38 Asset Archaeology pieces, 9 technical guides, 10 newsletter
editions and 1 case study. There is no article text in the handover.

SMEC wrote all of them, so this is not a permissions problem. But as things
stand every one of them builds LinkedIn's authority rather than SMEC's. The
straightforward fix is republishing on `smecoilandgas.com` with the canonical
pointing here and LinkedIn treated as syndication — which needs the source
text, and a decision.

Until then those pages can launch excluded from search, which is better than
publishing link lists.
*Blocks: whether Projects & Resources is a content cluster or a bookmark
page.*

**5.6 Who is running search after launch, and can they edit the pages?**
Two halves of one question. If an agency is involved they need to be in the
conversation before 5.1 and 5.3 are settled, not introduced afterwards.

And whoever it is will want to change titles, meta descriptions and H1s
continuously — that is most of the job. Today those are typed data in `lib/`
and every change needs a developer and a deploy. That is 1.4, and a search
rebuild turns it from a handover checkbox into the thing that decides whether
any of this is maintainable.
*Blocks: nothing at launch. Decides whether the site can be worked on after
it.*

**5.7 What does success look like, and over what period?**
Three things worth agreeing in writing now rather than explaining later: that
technical B2B search is a six-to-twelve month story rather than a six-week
one; that a same-domain replacement normally dips before it recovers, even
when the migration is clean; and what is actually being measured — rankings,
organic sessions, or RFQ submissions. The last of those is the only one that
pays for anything, and it is already instrumented (4.1).
*Blocks: nothing. Prevents an argument in December.*

**5.8 UAE/GCC and India — one site, or two audiences?**
34 of the 38 Asset Archaeology articles are India-focused, on a site whose
homepage leads with Abu Dhabi, for a company whose India entity is a separate
legal entity with its own phone number and mailbox. 1.5 currently assumes
English-only with no hreflang and no country pages.

If India is a real target market rather than a source of content, that is a
structural decision — a section, a subdomain, or out of scope — and it is
cheaper to make now than after the architecture freezes.
*Blocks: nothing today. Overlaps 5.1 and 1.5.*

## The four that actually hold up a launch

1. Where an RFQ lands (1.2)
2. Hosting, and staging protection (1.3)
3. Client logos and the counters — approve them or take them down (2.1, 2.2)
4. Privacy and consent wording (4.2)

The domain is settled. Everything else on this list makes the site better or
closes a checklist row; these four decide whether it can go live at all.

## The two that expire

Different from the four above: nothing stops without them, but the window
closes and does not reopen.

1. **The Search Console export (5.2).** Sixteen months of history, and the
   pre-launch snapshot only exists if someone takes it before the switch.
2. **The `/wp-content/uploads/` copy (4.4).** Possible only while the current
   site is still serving. 203 original images, 186 of them uploaded in 2025
   and 2026.

Both are an hour's work on the right day and impossible on the wrong one.

## The one that causes rework

**Keyword validation versus the route freeze (5.1).** Everything structural —
the URL map, the redirect map, the sitemap, every internal link — is
downstream of it. It is cheap to answer this week and expensive to answer
after the routes are written.
