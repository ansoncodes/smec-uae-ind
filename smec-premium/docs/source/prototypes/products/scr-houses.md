# SCR Houses for Onshore & Offshore Drilling Rigs

Reliable DC drive power for demanding drilling operations. Integrated SCR conversion, motor controls, protection and cooling in one engineered package.

## SCR Houses for Drilling Power That Cannot Afford an Unplanned Stop _(hidden in prototype)_

An SCR house is more than a shelter for power electronics. It is the interface between rig generation, DC drive loads, protection and the drilling sequence. SMEC engineers SCR power packages around the existing electrical architecture and the practical realities of offshore and onshore rigs.


## How it works

**Incoming power and conversion**

Incoming AC distribution, transformer secondaries and controlled six-pulse or multi-pulse thyristor bridges must be coordinated with generator fault levels and rig load steps. Bridge topology and any harmonic mitigation follow the electrical study.

**DC output and motors**

SCR output can be configured for DC drive applications, including 600–1,200 V DC arrangements. Output voltage, armature current, field supply and braking are matched to the connected motors and drilling duty.

**Controls and protection**

Firing-angle control, current limits, bridge cooling, DC contactors, protective interlocks and drive permissives interface with drawworks, mud pumps and other DC drives. Regeneration or dynamic braking depends on the complete drive and power-system topology.

**Enclosure and utilities**

MCC feeders, engine/generator controls, ventilation, cable routing, segregation, earthing, maintenance access and transport mounts are specified together.


## Key components

- Generator and transformer interface. The generator connection must accommodate voltage/frequency variation, transient load steps, breaker status and coordinated protection. Interface signals should be identified in the single-line diagram and I/O schedule.
- Rectifier bridges and firing control. Controlled thyristor bridges regulate DC output through firing-angle control; bridge grouping, cooling, semiconductor protection and feedback must match the motor duty.
- DC bus and drive feeders. DC distribution, isolation and feeder protection must be sized for continuous current, peak demand and fault-clearing requirements, with safe maintenance isolation.
- Current and voltage feedback. Current and voltage transducers support closed-loop regulation, overload limitation, fault detection and calibrated operator indication.
- Cooling and ventilation. Ventilation and HVAC must remove the heat produced by power electronics and auxiliaries at the specified site ambient; loss-of-cooling alarms and interlocks should be included.
- Protective interlocks. Permissives and trips prevent unsafe sequencing, such as enabling a drive without cooling, lubrication, breaker readiness or the required machinery state.
- PLC/HMI alarms. PLC/HMI functions present status, operating modes, permissives, faults and event history. Commands and safety-related shutdown functions must remain correctly separated.
- Earthing and cable segregation. Protective earthing, bonding, power/control cable segregation and shield termination reduce shock risk and electrical interference.

## Upgrades & retrofits

- Retain the enclosure and replace obsolete SCR firing cards, bridges or DC contactors after a thermal and insulation survey.
- Modernise generator interfaces, alarm logic and HMI while preserving verified motor protection.
- Assess an SCR-to-VFD conversion only alongside motor replacement, braking duty, harmonics and generator capacity.
Retrofit scope depends on existing OEM interfaces, component condition, available space, shutdown windows and the approval basis of any safety-related functions.

## FAQs

**Can an existing SCR house be upgraded without replacing the entire enclosure?**

Often the enclosure and some auxiliaries can be retained after condition, space, cooling and compliance review.

**What information is needed for an SCR house RFQ?**

Single-line diagram, generator details, motor loads, existing SCR details, drawings, operating voltage and site constraints.

**Is an SCR house the same as a VFD house?**

No. SCR houses commonly supply controlled DC drive systems; VFD houses typically control AC motors.


## Specifications table

| Specification | Technical description / design basis |
|---|---|
| SCR topology and bridge configuration | Specify the number of controlled rectifier bridges, pulse arrangement, firing controls and DC feeder allocation. Six-pulse and multi-pulse arrangements have different transformer and harmonic implications. |
| Input supply and short-circuit level | Record generator or utility voltage/frequency, transformer impedance and maximum/minimum fault levels. These govern semiconductor protection, switchgear withstand and fault-clearing coordination. |
| Connected DC motor ratings | List each DC motor armature/field voltage, continuous and peak current, duty cycle, speed range and braking duty; bridge sizing must follow actual drawworks and mud-pump demand. |
| Harmonic and power-quality considerations | Assess rectifier current distortion, generator loading and voltage notching; mitigation may require transformer phase shifting, reactors or filters after a power-system study. |
| Thermal duty | Calculate losses from SCR bridges, buswork and auxiliaries at maximum duty and ambient temperature; specify ventilation capacity, filtration, alarms and cooling redundancy if needed. |
| Hazardous-area boundary | Determine whether the house is in a classified or non-classified location and identify any certified field interfaces, pressurisation requirements or installation restrictions. |
| Access and maintainability | Provide working clearance, isolation points, removable modules, lifting access and safe cable-entry routes so technicians can inspect bridges, busbars and cooling equipment. |
