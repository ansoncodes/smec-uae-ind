# 4. Procurement as a Service (PaaS) — New Commercial Pillar

## H1: Engineering-Led Procurement for Oil & Gas, Offshore & Industrial Assets
Procurement becomes an engineering problem when a shutdown depends on an obsolete PLC card, a rig needs a replacement drive before mobilization, an OEM lead time exceeds the project window, or a proposed substitute has to work with an existing control, electrical or hazardous-area architecture. SMEC Procurement as a Service is designed for those requirements.
The service should not be positioned as general trading. SMEC can combine requirement validation, technical identification, sourcing, lifecycle review, equivalency assessment, documentation, expediting and — where required — engineering integration and commissioning. The objective is to reduce the gap between “a part was purchased” and “the asset can actually use it.”

## PaaS workflow
RFQ / BOM / nameplate → technical identification → criticality & lifecycle check → OEM / approved sourcing routes → technical bid evaluation → equivalency / interface review → commercial comparison → documentation & inspection → expediting / logistics coordination → integration / commissioning support where required

Critical engineering rule: an alternative part is not accepted as interchangeable merely because its nominal rating or description appears similar. Form, fit, function, electrical characteristics, I/O, firmware/software, communications, environmental rating, hazardous-area certification, safety function and project/client requirements must be checked as applicable.

# 5. Offshore Drilling — Flagship Deep-Content Page

## H1: Offshore Drilling Rig Electrical, Control & Safety Systems
An offshore drilling rig is not one automation system. It is a collection of power-generation equipment, electrical distribution, drives, drilling machinery, well-control equipment, safety systems, communications, instrumentation and auxiliary packages that must operate as one asset. A jack-up, semi-submersible or drillship may combine equipment from several OEMs and several generations of technology, with later modifications only partly reflected in drawings and software records.
That is why a seemingly isolated problem — an obsolete PLC, unavailable SCR card, unstable HMI, recurring jacking alarm, failed communication link or ageing gas-detection package — can become a rig-level engineering problem. The component cannot be assessed only by its part number; its power, control, safety, software, communication and operating interfaces have to be understood.
SMEC supports offshore drilling assets at these interfaces. Scope can begin with troubleshooting or replacement of one subsystem and expand into engineering, procurement, control migration, panel/package manufacture, multi-OEM integration, FAT, mobilization, installation, SAT, commissioning and a wider rig-reactivation or modernization programme.

## Understand the rig as an operating chain
POWER GENERATION → DISTRIBUTION → SCR/VFD DRIVES → DRILLING MACHINERY → CONTROL & INTERLOCKS → WELL CONTROL & SAFETY → MONITORING → COMMUNICATIONS → DIGITAL / REMOTE VISIBILITY
A change at one layer can alter several others. Replacing a drive can change protection, cooling, motor, control and PLC interfaces. Replacing a PLC can affect I/O, interlocks, HMI tags, communications, software backups and cutover strategy. Reactivating a stacked rig therefore requires an installed-base view rather than a list of replacement parts.
